## Aim & scope

Covers all oceanography sub-disciplines (biological, physical, chemical oceanography; sedimentology; geology; marine conservation), coastal to open sea, with emphasis on interdisciplinary, process-oriented work. Geography is not restricted to Brazil/South America — case studies from anywhere are accepted. Taxonomy/morphology/species-occurrence papers are only considered in Special Article Collections, which isn't this paper's category, so no issue there.

**Geopolitical naming note:** the journal recommends *not* naming specific states/provinces/countries in titles, preferring geo-referenced framing. Current title ("...Across 5 Islands") already complies — no country names in the title.

## Manuscript type: Original Article (this paper's category)

Required section order: **Abstract → Keywords → Introduction → Material and Methods → Results → Discussion → Conclusions → AI Use Disclosure → Data Availability Statement → Supplementary Material → Acknowledgements → Funding → Author Contributions → Conflicts of Interest → References → Tables → Figures.**

- No word limit, but must be concise.
- Results and Discussion must **not** be merged into one section.
- Line numbers required, starting right after Keywords (added at final formatting, not needed for the draft).

## First-page / front-matter requirements

- **Title**: centered, bold, concise; scientific names italicized.
- **Running Title**: max 6 words, max 60 characters including spaces.
- **Author list + affiliations**: full name(s); ORCID mandatory for every author; corresponding author marked with an asterisk; affiliation must include a complete postal address (city, postal code, state/province, country).
- **Conflict of interest declaration** on the first page too (in addition to the full Conflicts of Interest section near the end).

## Abstract & Keywords

- Abstract: **max 300 words**, no subheadings, no citations, must cover objective/hypothesis, summarized methods, key results, and concluding remarks.
- Keywords: **max 5**, comma-separated, no country/state/season names, no abbreviations, nothing already in the title.

## AI Use Disclosure — exact required wording

Must state which of these applies, using their template language:

- No AI used: *"The authors declare that no generative artificial intelligence (AI) tools were used in the preparation, writing, or editing of this manuscript."*
- AI for language only: *"Artificial intelligence tools (list all used, e.g., ChatGPT, OpenAI) were used exclusively to refine the English language of this manuscript. The content was carefully reviewed by the authors to ensure consistency and correctness, and the authors are fully responsible for the final version of the manuscript."*
- AI for other generative use (this paper's case — coding-assistant help + prose review): must name every tool and specify exactly where/how it was used, plus: *"The authors carefully reviewed all AI-generated outputs and are fully responsible for their accuracy and interpretation."*

**Placement: this section goes in the main body, positioned between Conclusions and Data Availability Statement — not in Methods**, per the exact required sequence above. (One general policy paragraph elsewhere on the site says AI use should be "disclosed in the Methods section or its equivalent" — the structured section list is the more specific and authoritative instruction for where the actual AI Use Disclosure section sits in the manuscript.)

Also required, separately: AI tool use must be **declared in the cover letter** at submission — undisclosed use risks the manuscript being returned or rejected outright.

## References — Harvard style (not APA)

Key formatting differences from APA:
- Year is **not parenthesized** and follows a period after the initials, e.g. `Schneider, E. K. 2015. Title...` (not `Schneider, E. K. (2015). Title...`).
- Journal reference format: `Author, Initials. Year. Title. Journal Name, Volume, pages.` — no issue/parenthetical volume number.
- Alphabetical reference list order.
- In-text citations: `(Author, Year)`, multiple citations in chronological order.
- A Zotero/EndNote/CSL style file is provided by the journal to automate this.
- Thesis/dissertation/report references need an active URL + access date.

## Mandatory end-matter sections (all manuscript types)

- **Data Availability Statement** — must state where data lives, or *"All data are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request,"* or *"This study does not involve any data."*
- **Supplementary Material** — statement even if none; Zenodo recommended for hosting any supplementary files.
- **Acknowledgements** — brief.
- **Funding** — explicit statement: *"This research received no external funding"* or funder name + grant number.
- **Author Contributions** — CRediT taxonomy, using initials; **not required for single-author manuscripts** (this paper qualifies for the exemption).
- **Conflicts of Interest** — explicit statement, e.g. *"The authors declare no conflicts of interest."*

## Figures & Tables

- Minimum 1000px width/height or 300 dpi; preferred width 15–20cm; TIFF/JPEG/EPS preferred; 40MP upper limit.
- Tables: portrait orientation only (no landscape tables accepted).
- Uploaded as separate files from the main text at submission (not embedded in the main manuscript file) — figure captions go directly into the ScholarOne portal; table captions go in the same file as the table, before the table.
- All figures/tables numbered sequentially by order of appearance, self-explanatory captions, acronyms spelled out in captions even if already defined in text.

## Peer review mechanics

- Minimum 2 reviewers; verdict is accept / minor revision / major revision / reject.
- Revisions require a point-by-point rebuttal letter, plus **both** a clean revised file and a separate tracked-changes file.
- Generally only 2 rounds of revision; a 3rd round is not guaranteed.
- Manuscripts with weak English are rejected after 2 rounds even if the science is sound — professional language review recommended given English isn't the author's first pass-through language for formal publication.
